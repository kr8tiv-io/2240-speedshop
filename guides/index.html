Alberta numbers

The 2240 Speed Shop guides answer the three questions every classic owner in Alberta asks: what a restoration actually costs in Canadian dollars, what provincial law says about a modified vehicle, and what a −40 winter does to old iron. Three pillar guides, written from the shop floor in Edmonton.

Nobody local publishes this. American cost guides quote American money. National law articles average seven provinces into mush. Winter advice written in California is worth exactly what it costs. We build here, in the cold, for Canadian dollars, so these are the numbers and rules we actually work to.

Why does a speed shop write guides?

Because the quote goes down easier

A restoration number lands hard when it arrives cold. It lands fine when you already know a frame-off is a thousand-hour job. Reading the cost guide before the estimate is the shortest route to an honest conversation.

Because the rules are the rules

We would rather tell you what Alberta expects before the metal is cut than argue about it at inspection. A build that cannot be plated, insured, or driven is not a build. It is an expensive ornament.

Because winter is the real test

Anything runs in July. The shop standard is a car that starts at minus thirty and comes out of storage in April without a surprise. Everything we know about that came from doing it here, badly, until we got good at it.

Got a project? Let’s talk straight.

Photos in, honest scope out. If the answer is that your car is not worth what it would take, you will hear that too.
