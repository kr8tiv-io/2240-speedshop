## 1. Strategy summary

### 1.1 Positioning correction that shapes the selection

The blog-keyword research spans both modern-performance tuning AND classic/restoration content. The keyword universe (`keyword-universe.md` §1) settled the question: **2240 is a customs & classics restoration shop with performance capability — not a dyno/import-tuner shop.** The 50 below are therefore weighted hard toward the shop's actual identity (restoration, restomods, engine swaps, classic service, Alberta legality, winter survival) and away from head-to-head tuner content where specialists are entrenched (Eurotekk owns Euro, SSS/Ronin own JDM/Subaru, six shops own diesel performance).

**Deliberately excluded, with reasons (all grounded in the research):**
- **A5 (DPF/EGR deletes)** — Park Muffler already ranks and openly markets it; teardown-1 flags it as a compliance exposure "2240 should NOT copy." Diesel performance is "not 2240's fight" (keyword-universe §2).
- **A15 (E85 guide)** — the #2 raw opportunity, but it pre-sells *flex-fuel tuning* to an import/modern audience that isn't 2240's customer. The classic-owner fuel questions it touches (ethanol in old engines) are captured by entry #10 instead.
- **C1–C5, C9, G1 (staged-tune / platform-tune education)** — MED-HIGH difficulty against UK/national tuner content, and E-E-A-T requires a dyno program 2240 doesn't run (keyword-universe: dyno service page "only if a dyno actually exists; fine as blog" — we keep exactly one cost post, #18).
- **A14/G6 (JDM/RHD)** — SSS Motorsports' entrenched lane.
- **D5 (winter tires)** — MED-HIGH vs Fountain Tire/dealers; the RWD-winter entry (#30) captures the enthusiast slice.

- **Authority pieces, 1,500–2,500w (27 posts):** big commercial-investigation and hub topics — cost guides, restomod/restoration decision content, buyer guides, the legality pillar spokes.
- **Punchy answers, 800–1,200w (23 posts):** question queries with weak SERPs where a fast, complete answer wins (OOP-fail next steps, spring checklist, block heaters, carb symptoms, drone fix).

### 1.5 Pillar-and-cluster internal linking plan

Four hub (pillar) pages sit above the blog, per the research's structural recommendations (blog-keywords §I: three hubs; the fourth is the differentiation play, rec #5):

1. **P1 — "Classic Car Restoration in Alberta: The Complete Guide"** → spokes: #11, 12, 34–43, 48–50. Supports the **Restoration** and **Restomods & Custom Builds** service pages. (Matches the AI-SEO playbook's launch cornerstone.)
2. **P2 — "Modified & Classic Car Laws in Alberta"** → spokes: #1–9. Supports **Performance & Tuning** + **Engine & Swaps** service pages.
3. **P3 — "What It Really Costs in Canada (Edmonton, 2026)"** → spokes: #11–18. Supports every service page; refreshed annually each January ("(2027)" title refresh, research rec #3).
4. **P4 — "Alberta Winter Survival for Classics & Performance Cars"** → spokes: #24–31. Supports **Classic Service** + storage/prep offers.

**Linking rules (every post):** link UP to its pillar, ACROSS to 2–4 siblings (specified per entry), and DOWN to exactly one money page (the 6-page service architecture from keyword-universe §14: Restoration / Restomods & Custom Builds / Engine & Swaps / Performance & Tuning / Rust & Metalwork / Classic Service). Edmonton/Alberta in H1 or first paragraph where natural; minimum one real shop photo per post; CAD pricing everywhere (research rec #2).

**Schema policy (per the AI-SEO playbook §3.3):** `Article`/`BlogPosting` on every post with a real `Person` author; add `FAQPage` on legal and cost posts (visible text = schema text). **`HowTo` schema is deliberately NOT used** — Google killed HowTo rich results in 2023; how-to content is written as numbered steps under Article schema instead. Answer-first BLUF paragraphs, question-format H2s, and comparison tables throughout (playbook §7.3: tables earn 2.5–4.2× more AI citations).

**Competitive context:** the three most relevant vintage-niche rivals (Sideshow, Wadson's, Acceleration) have published a combined **two blog posts ever** (teardown-3), and the tier-A leaders' blogs are dead, promo-only, or frozen (teardown-1). Fifty fresh, dated, Edmonton-targeted posts enter a market with effectively no direct content competition.
